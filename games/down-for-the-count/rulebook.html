A wrestling card game · 2 to 5 players · about 15 minutes

DOWN FOR THE COUNT

Every card in your hand is a move you can still make. Run out of moves and you tap out. Run out of stamina and the ref counts you out. Either way you’re done for the round, and the last wrestler still on their feet wins it.

Object of the game: be the last wrestler standing. Each round scores by the order wrestlers drop out, and after three rounds the highest total wins the match.

In the box

60Cards, poker-sized: ranks 1 to 10, six of each, with the number in two corners. Each rank is a wrestling move, from a Chop at 1 to a Frog Splash at 10. 1Rulebook. Your stamina cards come from the same deck, so nothing else is needed.

Setup

  1. Build the deck for your player count (table) and shuffle.
  2. Deal each player a hand, plus three face-down cards in a row in front of them: your stamina. Don’t look at your stamina cards.
  3. With 2 players, set the last 12 cards aside unseen for the round. Otherwise the whole deck is dealt.
  4. Pick who opens the first round. After that, the previous round’s winner opens.

Arrange your hand however you like, whenever you like.

Moves

You play cards in groups of 1 to 4 called moves:

  • Sets: cards of the same rank. One card on its own is a set of 1.
  • Runs: 2 to 4 cards in consecutive ranks, one of each.

A run of 3

Answering a move

To play on top of the move on the table, yours has to beat it. More cards always wins. Same number of cards? Higher wins.

››

In full, the first rule that applies decides:

  1. More cards always wins, whatever the type or numbers.
  2. Same number of cards: a set beats a run. A pair beats a 2-run; three 6s beat 4-5-6.
  3. Same number and type: higher wins. Runs compare by their top card.

5-5 is on the table: beat it with a higher pair, or any move of 3 or 4 cards.

5-6-7 is on the table: beat it with any three of a kind, a 3-run topping out on 8, 9 or 10, or any 4-card move.

Your turn

If the table is empty, you open: play any move. You can’t skip this.

Otherwise, do one of three things:

  • Play a move that beats the one on the table.
  • Reversal: take one card from the move on the table into your hand, and discard one of your stamina cards face down.
  • Kick out: take one of your stamina cards into your hand. The move on the table stays.

A reversal or a kick out uses up one stamina: you survive, you’re a card richer, and you’re one step closer to the pin. With 2 players there is no kick out. If you can’t beat the move, you reverse.

Stamina

Your face-down cards are your stamina. Every reversal and every kick out uses one up. Once they’re gone, the next time you’d have to reverse or kick out, you’re pinned: out of the round. A stamina card that comes into your hand is just a card. It never goes back.

If you play your last card and your hand is empty, you’ve tapped out: also out of the round. Pinned or tapped out, it’s the same result.

Reversals

A reversal takes one card off the move on the table, and whatever’s left must still be a legal move:

  • From a set, take any one card. Four of a kind becomes three, a pair becomes a single.
  • From a run, take either end card. A 4-run becomes a 3-run, a 2-run a single.
  • Reversing a single clears the table. The next player opens.

When your move comes back

If the turn comes back around to you and the move on the table is still yours:

  • If it was reversed (it’s smaller than you played it), it’s live. Beat it, reverse it or kick out, same as anyone.
  • If nobody touched it (everyone kicked out), take one card from it back into your hand for free: any card from a set, an end card from a run. The next player then answers what’s left. If that was the last card, the table clears and the next player opens.

If the player who played the current move is out, play continues against it as normal; once it has gone all the way around, the next player opens.

Winning a round

Wrestlers drop out one by one until a single player is left standing. They win the round.

Example of play

Three players, Ana, Ben and Cy, each have three stamina cards.

  1. Ana opens with 5-5.
  2. Ben plays 7-7: same size and type, higher.
  3. Cy reverses. He takes one 7 into his hand and discards a stamina card. The move is now a single 7.
  4. Ana plays a 9, a higher single.
  5. Ben kicks out: he takes one of his stamina cards into his hand. The 9 stays.
  6. Cy plays 3-3. More cards always wins.
  7. Ana plays 4-5-6, three cards over two.
  8. Ben and Cy both kick out. Cy is down to his last stamina card.
  9. The turn is back to Ana and nobody touched her 4-5-6, so she takes the 6 back into her hand for free. Ben must now answer 4-5.

Quick reference

Empty table: open with any move of 1–4 cards.

Otherwise: play a move that beats it, reverse (take a card off it, discard a stamina card) or kick out (take a stamina card into your hand). No kick out with 2 players.

What beats what: more cards; then a set over a run; then higher.

Your move comes back untouched: take one card from it back.

Out: empty hand (tapped out) or no stamina when you need it (pinned). Last one standing wins the round.

Ringside advice

  • Your cards are your moves. Every card you play is one you can’t play later. The minimum that beats the move is usually right.
  • Stamina is cheaper than it looks, until it isn’t. Each stamina card buys a card. The one after your last is the pin.
  • Reverse when the card helps you; kick out when the move should stay scary for the next player. But if everyone kicks out, the leader gets a card back for free.
  • Save the big one for when they’re on the ropes. A move nobody can answer is a finisher against a wrestler with no stamina left, and a gift against anyone else.
  • Watch the table. Six of every rank exist. Count what’s been played.
