SENTAI

A cooperative card game for 2–4 players, 20–30 minutes. Nobody talks about their hand, and nobody ever draws. Hold the line together.

by Darren Don
The goal. You are a squad of Heroes, each piloting a machine. Together, beat every monster the Boss sends across three acts. Your hand is your health: nobody ever draws, and if any Hero’s hand runs out, the whole squad loses.

Components

Setup

1. Choose a Boss. Emperor Gorvax is the place to start. Sort his 18 cards into three decks by their backs: Act I, Act II and Act III.

2. Shuffle each act’s deck on its own and draw cards from it, face down, without looking. Return the rest to the box.

3. Use one more color than you have Heroes (any colors). Shuffle those Hero cards and deal 15 to each Hero. Any cards left over go back in the box, unseen.

4. Lay the Act I monsters face up in a line, in the order drawn. The later acts stay face down.

5. Pick a Hero at random to be the first lead.

Reading a Boss card

Every Boss card names a combo and prints its size three times: for 2, 3 and 4 Heroes (the dots). Use the number for your squad.

The four combos

ComboCards that fit
RunDifferent ranks, any colors, that all fit in a row as long as the number. Gaps are fine while you build: a Run of 3 can hold a 5 and a 7, waiting for the 6.
SetAll the same rank.
ColorAll the same color, any ranks.
CombineAll different colors, any ranks.

The first card played starts the combo, and every card after it has to fit with the cards already there.

No table talk. Never say or hint at what you hold, what you want played, or what you plan to do. Everything the squad learns comes from the cards.

A fight

Fight the monsters in the line one at a time, from the front.

  1. Build the comboStarting with the lead and going left, each Hero takes one turn: add 1 or 2 cards that fit to the shared combo, face up, or pass. Stop as soon as the combo reaches the monster’s number.
    A Run of 3: a 4, a 5 and a 6, in any colors.
  2. BeatenIf the combo is complete, the monster is beaten. Discard it and every card in the combo: those cards are spent.
  3. Or missedIf everyone has had a turn and the combo is short, the monster holds on. Every card in the combo goes back to the Hero who played it, so everyone now knows who holds those cards. Then every Hero discards 1 card of their choice, face up.
  4. Pass the leadThe lead passes to the left. After a miss, fight the same monster again. After a win, fight the next one in the line.

When the line is empty, the act is over. Lay the next act’s monsters face up in a line and carry on.

Winning and losing

The squad wins by beating the last monster of Act III.

The squad loses the moment any Hero has no cards in hand. You may never add your last card to a combo, so a hand only runs out by discarding.

What you know

You can see this act’s monsters, every card in the combo, every discard, and how many cards each Hero holds. When a combo falls short you also see exactly which cards go back to which Hero: remember them.

You never see the cards left in the box, your teammates’ hands, or the monsters of the acts to come.

Playing well

The lead’s first card is a message: it picks the rank or the color, so lead something you think the others can follow. Adding a card to a combo that then falls short costs you nothing extra, because the card comes back. Holding back only saves cards when the squad would have won without you.

A missed monster never goes away. It costs the squad a card each and then has to be beaten anyway, so missing on purpose never helps.

Easier or harder. Add the Hero powers on the next page for an easier game. For a harder one, face Vael, the Hydra.

Questions

Do I have to add a card if I can? No. You may always pass.

Can a Run go around from 12 to 1? No. 12 is the highest rank and 1 the lowest.

Can a Combine be bigger than the colors in play? No card asks for that: the largest Combine is one card of every color.

May I look through the discards? Yes. Discards are face up and anyone may look at them at any time.

Vael, the Hydra

A harder Boss. Set up as usual with Vael’s 18 cards, but deal 14 cards to each Hero.

Grow two heads from the Act I pile with 2 Heroes, or three heads with 3 or 4 Heroes, side by side and face up. The rest of each act stays face down. Each head has a hex, in force from the moment the head appears until it is gone.

  1. A roundStarting with the lead, each Hero takes one turn: add 1 or 2 cards to one head, or 1 card each to two heads, or pass. Cards stay on a head from round to round.
  2. CutAt the end of the round, a head whose combo is complete is cut off. Discard it and its cards.
  3. EnrageAn unfinished head that is upright turns sideways: it is enraged.
  4. StrikeAn unfinished head that was already enraged strikes. Discard it and the cards on it, and every Hero discards 2 cards.
  5. GrowFill each empty space with a new head from the current act’s pile, then the next act’s. Pass the lead to the left.

The squad wins when the last head is cut off or strikes, as long as every Hero still holds a card.

The hexes

HexWhile this head is out
HungerAt the end of each round, the Hero with the most cards discards 1. On a tie, the tied Hero nearest the lead, going left.
VenomIf this head strikes, every Hero discards 1 more.
CoilNo Hero may add more than 1 card a turn to this head.
SplitYou can’t feed this head if the Hero just before you fed it.
BroodWhen this head is enraged, it needs 1 more card.
SpiteWhen this head is cut, the Hero who added its last card discards 1.

Hero powers (optional)

Shuffle the five power cards and deal one to each Hero, face up.

PowerWhat it does
CommandAlways on. When you lead, you may add up to 3 cards.
RiptideOnce a game. When the squad would discard, nobody does.
OverchargeOnce a game. One card you add fits the combo, whatever its rank and color.
SteadyAlways on. Whenever the squad discards, you discard 1 fewer.
BeaconOnce an act. On your turn, show one card from your hand to everyone.
