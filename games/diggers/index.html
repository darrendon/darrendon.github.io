About the game

It’s 1877, during the Bone Wars. Each pair of neighboring camps shares a dig site, and the site belongs to whoever played the best find there last. You pick up your cards in the order they’re dealt and never rearrange them, so you can only play cards that already sit side by side. To fix your hand, you can poach the end card of any find beside your camp, yours or a rival’s. Beat a rival’s find and you keep its cards. When anyone empties their hand, the season ends: your captured cards and the sites you hold score, and every card still in your hand counts against you.

Now or later

Every play is a tradeoff between grabbing a site now and keeping the cards you need later in the order you hold them.

Poaching cuts both ways

Every poach sends one of your cards to the player across that site, so you have to give away a card they can’t use.

Small box, short game

45 cards and six site cards in a tuck box, for 2–4 players in 10–20 minutes.

When someone plays their last card, the season ends immediately. Beaten rival finds you kept and sites you hold score; cards still in your hand count against you. Play one season per player.

Five skeletons, nine bones each

Each card shows part of one of five skeletons, with its own rock color and symbol. Lay a species’ nine cards in a grid, 1 2 3 / 4 5 6 / 7 8 9, and they form the whole skeleton. Two players use four species; three or four use all five.

The dig sites

Six real quarries from the Bone Wars

Each site is a half-size card laid between two camps, and your finds go on your side of it. They are named for quarries where Cope’s and Marsh’s crews competed.

Advanced: site effects. Every site card has a second side with a rule of its own. Once you know the game, shuffle the six each season and deal them effect side up, so the same hand plays differently at each site.

Pick your cards up in the order dealt and never rearrange them.

Finds

A find is one or more cards sitting next to each other in your hand: a single card, a run of consecutive numbers in order (3-4-5 or 5-4-3), or a set of one number, up to 4 cards. More cards beat fewer. At equal size, a suited run (every card one species) beats a set, and a set beats a run. Then the higher total wins.

Excavate

Lay a find at a site beside your camp. An empty site takes any find; a claimed site, yours or a rival’s, needs a find that beats the one there. The site is now yours. Beat a rival’s find and you keep its cards face down, 1 prestige each; your own old find is discarded.

Poach

Take one end card of any find at a site beside your camp, yours or a rival’s, and insert it anywhere in your hand. Then send any card from your hand to the digger across that site. They insert it anywhere, or turn it away and it’s out for the season.

Scoring

As soon as any player’s hand is empty, the season ends. Score +1 for each card you captured, +1 for each site you hold and −1 for each card left in your hand. Everything is reshuffled, fresh hands are dealt, and the Expedition Leader card passes left.

Winning

Play one season per player: two, three or four. Most prestige wins. A tie goes to whoever holds the strongest find at a dig site; if that ties too, share the win.

Advanced: site effects

Each season, shuffle the six site cards and deal them effect side up. Hard Rock and Shallow Pit limit the size of finds, Trackway ranks runs above sets, Overturned Beds lets the lower total win, at Shared Claim a tie takes the site, and at Open Pit a poacher discards a card instead of sending one.
