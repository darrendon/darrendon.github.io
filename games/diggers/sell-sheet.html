The game

Rival paleontologists work the same badlands. Between every pair of neighboring camps is a dig site, and it belongs to whoever brought up the finer find there last. Play runs or sets from a hand you may never sort, poach bones from the sites beside you, and hand a specimen across in return. Three field seasons; most prestige wins.

Why it works

  • A hand you can't sort. Only cards that sit side by side can be played together, so every turn is a small puzzle. Poaching lets you slot exactly the bone you need into the gap.
  • Every site is shared. Every find you lay is a target. The specimen you send back for a poached bone can be a gift or dead weight.
  • A built-in arc. Sites are worth 1, then 2, then 4 prestige, so the last season outweighs the first two. In simulated games the final season overturns the leader about a third of the time.
  • Six skeletons. Each species' nine cards assemble into a fossil slab, and a suited run, all from one skeleton, outranks any set its size.
