ABDUCTED

A social deduction game for 5–12 players, about 15 minutes. Everyone knows which side you’re on except you.

by Darren Don
The goal. Score points for your side, Human or Alien, by getting the beamed-up half to vote your way. The first side to 5 points wins, and you win if your card shows that side when the game ends. The catch: your own card faces away from you.

Components

Also: 12 plastic card stands.

Setup

1. With N players, take N Human cards and N Alien cards and shuffle them together. Return the rest to the box.

2. Deal one card face down to each player. Without looking at it, put it in your stand so it faces everyone else. You can read every other player’s card, never your own.

3. Put the other N cards face down in a pile in the middle. Nobody knows what’s in it.

4. Give everyone a split card. Put both cubes on 0 of the score card.

5. A random player takes the Mothership and leads the first turn.

Cards by player count

Always N of each: 5 players use 5 Human and 5 Alien cards, 12 players use all 24. Half the cards you use are dealt, and the other half wait in the middle.

Table talk. Say anything, at any time: tell people what they are, lie, make deals, accuse. The one rule: never show anyone their own card.

Your card

The card in your stand is your side. It can change during the game, whenever someone possesses you, and you won’t see it happen. Only the card in your stand at the end decides whether you win.

A turn

  1. PossessThe leader must possess another player. Take a random card from the middle and swap it into that player’s stand without letting them see it, then shuffle their old card into the middle. They might have just changed sides.
  2. SplitThe leader splits the whole table, themselves included, into two halves as evenly as possible, telling each player which side of their split card to show: Saucer or Cow.
    With 7 players, one half has 3 and the other 4.
  3. Beam upThe player to the leader’s left picks which half is beamed up. Then they flip the top card of the middle face up for everyone to see. It stays face up, out of the game.
  4. ThumbsOn three, everyone in the beamed-up half votes at once: thumbs up for Human, thumbs down for Alien. The majority scores 1 point for that side. A tie scores nothing.
  5. Pass the Mothershipto the left. That player leads the next turn.

The leader’s choices

Possess someone whose card helps you if it flips. Split the table so the people you trust land together. The player who picks the half has the last word, so the leader can’t control everything.

Winning

The game ends the moment a side reaches 5 points. Everyone takes their card out of its stand and looks at it. If your card shows the winning side, you win, along with everyone else on that side. It doesn’t matter which side you voted for.

What you know

The deck always holds the same number of Humans and Aliens. You can see every card in a stand except yours, plus every card flipped from the middle, so you can count. But the middle is never all face up, so counting gets you close and never certain. The rest comes from what people tell you, and how they vote.

Why vote one way

You want your side to score, so vote for the side you believe you’re on. If you’ve got it wrong, you’re scoring for the other team.

Everyone else knows what you are. Your teammates want you to know it; your opponents want you to believe the opposite. Listen to who says what, and remember that a possession can turn a friend into an enemy without either of you knowing.

Shorter or longer games

Play to 4 points for a quick game, or 6 for a longer one.

Playing online? Abducted is playable in your browser at darrendon.com/games/abducted, with a tutorial, bots, and online tables you can join with a five-letter code. Bots count cards, and will tell you what you are, truthfully or not.

Questions

Can I possess myself? No. The leader must possess another player, every turn.

What if the middle is empty? Skip the possession or the flip, whichever needs a card.

Can I tell someone what their card is? Yes, truthfully or not. You just can’t show them.

What does the flipped card tell me? It’s one fewer unknown card. If it’s an Alien, there’s one fewer Alien left that could be in your stand.

Does voting reveal anything? Only what each voter believes. Watch it: someone voting Human over and over thinks they’re Human, which tells you who has been talking to them.
