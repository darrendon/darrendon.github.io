Too Many Crooks!

A dice negotiation game for 3–6 players. Your crew comes from the dice your rivals send you, and you pay them with the dice you kept.

Overview

The pitch

Everyone rolls in the open, then takes turns sending four dice to rivals’ jobs. The dice you keep are the only money you have to pay your own crew. Each player builds a crew of one of each kind from the dice they were sent, then everyone pays their cuts in secret and reveals at once. Deals are made out loud, and none of them are binding. There are three jobs, so how you paid people on one affects the next.

One pool of dice

The dice you send out become your rivals’ crews, and the dice you keep pay your own. Send your best dice out and you have little left to pay anyone with.

One of each kind

A crew needs an inside man (1–2), a getaway driver (3–4) and the muscle (5–6). Every job needs a low die, and a host who already has a 6 has no use for yours.

Open deals, secret payouts

Players negotiate across the table. Cuts are placed behind your hand and revealed together, so everyone sees who kept their word.

20–30 minutes

Three jobs and a final reveal.

Components

Crew sheets and dice

Each crew sheet is named for a different heist. They all play the same; the names give the table something to call each job.

Rules in brief

How it plays

The full four-page rulebook.

Download the rules (PDF)View

Roll

Everyone rolls at once, in the open: 8 dice on the first job, 7 on the second, 6 on the third. Dice stay face up for the whole job.

Send your people out

In turn, send exactly four of your dice to rivals’ jobs, split however you like. You can’t send dice to your own job. The dice you keep are the only thing you can pay your crew with.

Pick your crew

Every crew is one of each kind: an inside man (a 1 or 2), a getaway driver (3 or 4) and the muscle (5 or 6). Never two of a kind. You score the total of your crew. Dice you don't take go back to their owners and earn nothing.

Hand out the cuts

Everyone secretly assigns their kept dice to their crew, then all reveal together. You pay three cuts on job 1 and two on jobs 2 and 3, so from job 2 on, one of your crew gets nothing and you choose who. You can stack several dice on one crook.

Your cut

Whoever receives a die scores it. On jobs 1 and 2 you have one kept die left over: score it, then return it to the box. That’s why you roll one fewer die each job.

The last job

On job 3 your crew scores double and both your kept dice are paid out, so there’s no die left over. Highest total after three jobs wins; ties go to the higher job-3 score.

Nobody shows up

If nobody sends you anything, run the job with your own people: take one of each kind from your kept dice and score them as your crew (doubled on job 3). You pay no one, and on jobs 1 and 2 you still take your cut from what's left.
