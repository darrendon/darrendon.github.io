Too Many Crooks!

A dice negotiation game for 3–6 players. You can't use your own people on a job, so you crew up from the dice your rivals send over — and pay them from whatever you kept back.

Overview

The pitch

Everyone rolls in the open. In turn, you send four of your dice to rivals' jobs; what you didn't send is what you pay people with. Each host crews up with one of each kind from what arrived, then everyone hands out their cuts at once and in secret. You can promise anything. None of it is binding. There are two more jobs after this one, and everybody chooses who they send to next time.

One pool, three jobs

The same dice are your workers, your wages and your score. Send your best people and there is nothing left to pay anyone with.

One of each kind

A crew needs an inside man, a driver and the muscle. A 1 is the die a job can't run without, not dead weight — and a second 6 is no use to a job that already has one.

Out loud, then in secret

Deals are made across the table. Cuts are placed behind your hand and revealed together, so the whole table sees exactly who kept their word.

Twenty-five minutes

Three jobs and a reveal. Short enough to play twice, long enough for a grudge.

Components

Crew sheets and dice

Every crew sheet is a different score. They all play the same — the names are there so the table has something to call each other's jobs.

Rules in brief

How it plays

The full rules, ready to read or print.

Download the rules (PDF)View

Roll

Everyone rolls at once, in the open: 8 dice on the first job, 7 on the second, 6 on the third. Dice stay face up all job, so everyone can see what everyone else has to offer.

Send your people out

In turn, send exactly four of your dice to rivals' jobs — any split, never your own. Whatever you didn't send, you keep. Those are the cuts you have to hand out, and the only thing you can pay anyone with.

Pick your crew

Every crew is one of each kind: an inside man (a 1 or 2), a getaway driver (3 or 4) and the muscle (5 or 6). Never two of a kind. You score the total of your crew. Dice you don't take go back to their owners and earn nothing.

Hand out the cuts

Everyone at once, in secret: assign your kept dice to your crew, then reveal together. Three cuts on the first job, two on the jobs after — so from job 2 on, one of your crew gets nothing, and you choose who. You may stack several dice on one person.

Your cut

Whoever receives a die scores it. On jobs 1 and 2 you'll have one kept die left over: score it, then put it back in the box — that contact has taken their money and gone, which is why you roll one fewer next job.

The last job

On job 3 your crew scores double and there's no die left over — both your kept dice go out. Highest total after three jobs wins, with ties going to the higher job-3 score. Nothing anyone said at any point was binding.

Nobody shows up

If nobody sends you anything, run the job with your own people: take one of each kind from your kept dice and score them as your crew (doubled on job 3). You pay no one, and on jobs 1 and 2 you still take your cut from what's left.
