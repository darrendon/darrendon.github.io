Mr. Magpie
Offerings before the first snow
A game by Darren Don

Mr. Magpie has a nest full of shiny things and a larder to fill before winter. The woodland critters can’t resist a sparkle, so every autumn they line up to trade him their acorns for his treasures.

He drives a hard bargain: he keeps every acorn you offer, whether you win or not. And when the first snow falls, the critter who has traded away the most food has to hand their finest treasure back for a meal.

3 to 5 playersAbout 20 minutesAuction and set collection

Objective

Score the most points from treasures and baubles before the first snow.

But watch your acorns. Whoever has the fewest left when the snow falls must hand their best treasure back to Mr. Magpie.

Getting ready

  1. Each critter takes a reference card and one set of acorn cards. Keep your acorn cards hidden in your hand. Return unused sets to the box.

  2. Shuffle the treasure cards into a face-down treasure deck. With 3 critters, first return the Disco Ball and the Tiara to the box.

  3. Shuffle the bauble cards into a face-down deck, the bag. Deal 8 baubles face up in a row beside it, from left to right.

  1. Shuffle the acorn deck and place it face down.

  2. Leave space for Mr. Magpie’s larder: a face-down pile where every offered acorn card ends up.

  3. Choose a start player at random.

Acorn cards

You pay for everything with acorn cards, and you can’t make change. To offer 7 you need cards that add up to at least 7, and every card you put down is spent.

Your hand is secret. Nobody may look at it or ask how much you have left.

Mr. Magpie’s deal

He keeps every acorn card anyone offers, whether they win the treasure or not.

But he never sends a guest home empty-handed. Drop out of an auction and he thanks you with one bauble for each acorn card you offered.

3

Playing the game

The game is a series of auctions, one for each treasure card. Every auction follows the same steps.

1Refresh the bauble row

Skip this for the very first treasure. After that, remove the leftmost bauble (the oldest), slide the rest left to close any gaps, and deal new baubles on the right until there are 8 again.

2Turn up a treasure

Turn up the top card of the treasure deck. This is what everyone is bidding for.

If it is a frost, run a secret auction instead: see page 6.

3Offer or pass

Starting with the start player and going clockwise, round and round, each critter chooses one:

Offer

Lay any number of acorn cards face up in front of you so that your total is higher than the top offering.

Cards you offered earlier in this auction still count. Nobody has to make the first offer.

Pass

You are out of this auction and can’t come back in.

If you hadn’t offered anything, draw a card from the acorn deck into your hand, without showing it.

If you had, you drop out. Leave your offering in front of you: see step 5.

The top offering is 5. Adding a 4 to the 3 you already put down makes 7, which beats it.

Two ways to shop. One big card can win a treasure by itself. Several small cards win more baubles when you drop out, as long as nobody leaves you holding a treasure you didn’t want.

4The last critter in wins

When everyone else has passed, the top offering takes the treasure, even if it was the only offer. The winner’s cards go to the larder and the winner takes no baubles.

The winner is the start player for the next treasure.

5Everyone who dropped out takes baubles

When the auction is over, everyone who dropped out puts their offering in the larder and takes one bauble from the row for each acorn card they offered. Keep them face up in front of you.

You must take one for every card, even if none of them helps you. If the row runs out, deal it back up to 8 and carry on.

The smallest offering takes all of theirs first, then the next smallest, and so on.

If nobody offers

The treasure waits face up, and whoever wins the next auction takes it too. Several treasures can wait at once. The start player moves one seat to the left.

Example: the Music Box

Mouse opens with a 3, and later adds a 4 to reach 7. When Hedgehog goes higher, Mouse drops out. Once the auction is over, both cards go to the larder and Mouse takes two baubles.

Hedgehog offers a 5, then adds a 2 and a 1 to reach 8. Everyone else has passed, so Hedgehog wins the Music Box. The three cards go to the larder, with no baubles.

Rabbit passes without offering anything, and draws a card from the acorn deck.

5

Frosts

Four of the treasure cards are frosts. A frost is a secret auction: everyone offers at once, and there is no chance to raise.

1Everyone chooses in secret

Refresh the bauble row as usual. Then everyone picks any acorn cards from their hand, or none, and holds them out face down.

2Reveal together

The biggest total wins the frost, along with any treasures that were waiting, and is the start player for the next treasure.

Everyone else who offered takes one bauble per card, smallest offering first, as in any auction. Ties go clockwise from the start player.

Anyone who offered nothing draws a card from the acorn deck. All offered cards go to the larder.

Tied for biggest?

Nobody wins. The frost waits face up like an unclaimed treasure, and whoever wins the next auction takes it too.

Everyone who offered still takes baubles, tied critters included, and all offered cards go to the larder.

Nobody offers?

The frost waits face up like any unclaimed treasure, and whoever wins the next auction takes it too.

The start player moves one seat to the left.

Example: the Crystal Brooch

Chipmunk reveals a 10 and a 2, for 12. That is the biggest total, so Chipmunk wins the frost.

Mole reveals a 6, a 2 and a 1, for 9. Mole offered three cards, so Mole takes three baubles.

Mouse offered nothing, and draws a card from the acorn deck.

6

The first snow

When the fourth frost is turned up, the snow falls and the game ends at once. That frost is not auctioned, and any treasures still waiting stay with Mr. Magpie.

  1. Count acorns. Everyone reveals their hand and adds it up. The critter with the fewest acorns hands their highest-scoring treasure back to Mr. Magpie. If several tie for fewest, they each hand one back. A critter with no treasures loses nothing, and if every critter is level, nobody hands anything back.

  2. Score. Everyone adds the points on the treasures they still have to the points from their baubles.

  3. Most points wins. If tied, the tied critter with more acorns wins. If that is tied too, they share the win.

Scoring baubles

Each kind of bauble scores as a set. The scoring is printed along the bottom of every card.

Earrings.

Berries.

Buttons.

Glass beads.

Feathers.

Bottle caps.

Count before you spend. Your finest treasure goes straight back to me if you have the fewest acorns when the snow falls. Watch what the others have offered, and keep a little back.

Questions

Can I offer more than I need to? Yes, and without change you often have to. More cards also mean more baubles if you drop out.

Can I come back in after passing? No. A pass lasts for that whole auction.

What if I have no acorn cards? Pass and draw from the acorn deck. A few passes and you can bid again.

Do I get baubles for a treasure I win? No. Baubles only go to critters who offered and didn’t win.

Do I have to take a bauble I don’t want? Yes. You take one for every acorn card you offered, even if it costs you points.

What if a deck runs out? If the bag is empty, shuffle the baubles removed from the row to make a new one. If the acorn deck is empty, nobody draws.

What is secret? Only your acorn hand. Treasures and baubles sit face up in front of you, and nobody may look through the larder.

Quick reference

  1. Refresh the row: the oldest bauble leaves, then deal back up to 8.
  2. Turn up a treasure. The last winner starts.
  3. Offer or pass. Beat the top offering with acorn cards, or pass. Pass with nothing offered: draw from the acorn deck.
  4. Last critter in wins the treasure and takes no baubles. If nobody offered, it waits for the next winner.
  5. Everyone who dropped out takes one bauble for each acorn card they offered, smallest offering first.
  6. Frost: everyone offers in secret. Biggest wins; if tied, nobody does.
  7. The fourth frost ends the game. Fewest acorns hands back their best treasure. Most points wins.
