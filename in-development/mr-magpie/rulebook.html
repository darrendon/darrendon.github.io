Mr. Magpie

An auction game for 3 to 5 critters, about 20 minutes. Trade your winter acorns for Mr. Magpie’s treasures. He keeps every acorn you offer, whether you win or not.

by Darren Don

The goal. Score the most points from treasures and baubles before the first snow.

The catch. Whoever has the fewest acorns left when the snow falls doesn’t make it through winter, and can’t win however much they collected.

What’s in the box

acorn cards in 5 setsOne set of for each critter: . Acorns are your money and your winter food.

-card acorn deck. You draw from it when you sit an auction out.

treasure cards treasures worth 5 to 15 points, and frosts worth 7 to 10. Frosts are auctioned in secret, and the last one ends the game.

bauble cards each of earrings, berries, buttons, feathers, bottle caps and glass beads. Each kind scores as a set, as printed on the card.

5 reference cardsOne for each critter: how the baubles score on one side, the steps of an auction on the other.

Mr. Magpie · rules1

Setup

  1. Each critter takes a reference card and one set of acorn cards. Keep your acorn cards hidden in your hand. Return unused sets to the box.

  2. Shuffle the treasure cards into a face-down treasure deck.

  3. Shuffle the bauble cards into a face-down deck, the bag. Deal 8 baubles face up in a row beside it, from left to right.

  4. Shuffle the acorn deck and place it face down.

  5. Leave space for Mr. Magpie’s larder: a face-down pile where every offered acorn card ends up.

  6. Choose a start player at random.

Acorn cards

You pay for everything with acorn cards, and you can’t make change. To offer 7 you need cards that add up to at least 7, and every card you put down is spent.

Your hand is secret: nobody may look at it or ask how much you have left. Everyone does see each card you offer.

Mr. Magpie’s deal

Mr. Magpie keeps every acorn card anyone offers, whether they win the treasure or not.

But he never sends a guest home empty-handed. Drop out of an auction and he thanks you with one bauble for each acorn card you offered.

Two ways to shop. One big card can win a treasure by itself. Several small cards win more baubles when you drop out, as long as nobody leaves you holding a treasure you didn’t want.
Mr. Magpie · rules2

Each treasure

  1. Refresh the row and show the treasure

    Skip the refresh for the first treasure. After that, remove the leftmost bauble (the oldest), slide the rest left to close any gaps, and deal new ones on the right until there are 8 again.

    Then turn up the top treasure card. For a frost, follow Frosts below instead of steps 2 to 4.

  2. Offer or pass

    Starting with the start player and going clockwise, round and round, each critter offers or passes.

    Offer: lay any number of acorn cards face up in front of you so that your total is higher than the top offering. Cards you offered earlier in this auction still count. Nobody has to make the first offer.

    Pass: you are out of this auction. If you pass without having offered anything, draw a card from the acorn deck into your hand, without showing it.

  3. Drop out and take baubles

    If you pass after offering, your cards go to the larder and you immediately take one bauble from the row for each acorn card you offered. Keep them face up in front of you.

    You must take one for every card, even if none of them helps you. If the row runs out, deal it back up to 8 and carry on.

  4. The last critter in wins

    When everyone else has passed, the top offering takes the treasure, even if it was the only offer. The winner’s cards go to the larder, they take no baubles, and they are the start player for the next treasure.

  5. If nobody offers

    The treasure waits face up, and whoever wins the next auction takes it too. Several can wait at once. The start player moves one seat to the left.

Example

The Music Box (10 points) is turned up. Mouse offers a 3. Hedgehog offers a 5. Rabbit passes without offering and draws from the acorn deck. Mouse adds a 4, for 7 in all. Hedgehog adds a 2 and a 1, for 8. Mouse passes, so Mouse’s two cards go to the larder and Mouse takes two baubles. Hedgehog wins the Music Box. Hedgehog’s three cards go to the larder, and Hedgehog starts the next auction.

Frosts

A frost is a secret auction. Everyone picks any acorn cards from their hand, or none, and holds them out face down. Then all reveal at once.

The biggest total wins the frost, along with any treasures that were waiting, and starts the next auction. Everyone else who offered takes one bauble per card, biggest offering first (ties go clockwise from the start player). Anyone who offered nothing draws from the acorn deck. All offered cards go to the larder. If nobody offers anything, the frost waits like an unclaimed treasure.

Tied for biggest? The tied critters offer again in secret, adding to what they already put down, until the tie breaks. If none of them adds a card, nobody wins: the frost waits like any unclaimed treasure, and everyone who offered takes baubles.

The first snow

When the fourth frost is turned up, the snow falls and the game ends at once. That frost is not auctioned, and any treasures still waiting stay with Mr. Magpie.

  1. Count acorns. Everyone reveals their hand and adds it up. The critter with the fewest acorns is out. If several tie for fewest, they are all out. (If every critter is level, nobody is out.)

  2. Score. Everyone still in adds the points on their treasures to the points from their baubles. A critter who is out still counts when you compare feathers and bottle caps.

  3. Most points wins. If tied, the tied critter with more acorns wins. If that is tied too, they share the win.

Questions

Can I offer more than I need to? Yes, and without change you often have to. More cards also mean more baubles if you drop out.

Can I come back in after passing? No. A pass lasts for that whole auction.

What if I have no acorn cards? Pass and draw from the acorn deck. A few passes and you can bid again.

Do I get baubles for a treasure I win? No. Baubles only go to critters who offered and didn’t win.

What if a deck runs out? If the bag is empty, shuffle the baubles removed from the row to make a new one. If the acorn deck is empty, nobody draws.

What is secret? Only your acorn hand. Treasures and baubles sit face up in front of you, and nobody may look through the larder.
