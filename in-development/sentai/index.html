Sentai

A cooperative card game. Every Hero’s cards are their part of the squad’s giant robot, and nobody ever draws, so every card you spend against a monster is one you never get back.

Players
2–4
Time
20–30 min
Genre
Cooperative · Shedding

How it plays

The rules in brief

  1. Deal the squad inUse one more color than there are Heroes, each color ranked 1–12, and deal everyone 14 cards. Each color is one part of the robot: the head and chest, an arm or a leg. From here on, no table talk.
  2. Line them upEach act shuffles its own deck of six monsters and draws a few, face down: 3, 3 and 4 with 2 Heroes, 3, 4 and 5 with 3, and 4 each with 4. When an act begins, lay its monsters face up in a line, so the squad can see that act’s fights coming but not the next act’s.
  3. Fight the next oneThe first monster in the act’s line wants a Run (ranks that fit in a row, any color, gaps allowed while building), a Set (one rank), a Color (one color, any ranks) or a Combine (all different colors), with a size printed for each squad size. Starting with the lead, each Hero adds 1 or 2 cards that fit, or passes.
  4. Beat it or lose itComplete the combo and the monster is defeated, and those cards are spent. Fall short and it gets away: every card goes back to the Hero who added it, so everyone knows who holds them, and every Hero discards 2 face up.
  5. Your hand is your healthNobody ever draws. The moment any Hero’s hand is empty, from playing or discarding, the Boss wins.
  6. Hold the lineThe lead passes left after every fight. Clear a line and the next act lines up. Get through Act III and the squad wins together.
  7. Or face the HydraSwap in Vael’s deck and two heads are out at once, each with a hex. Each turn add 1 or 2 cards to one head, or 1 card to each. Cards stay put. A head left standing enrages, and if it survives another round it strikes: its cards are lost and everyone discards 2.
  8. Add Hero powersOptionally, deal each Hero a power: Command, Riptide, Overcharge, Steady or Beacon. Each one is always on or used once.

What I’m looking at right now: Whether seeing each act’s monsters in order turns the end of the game into a plan instead of a coin flip, and whether seeing cards come back after a miss tells the squad enough about each other’s hands.
